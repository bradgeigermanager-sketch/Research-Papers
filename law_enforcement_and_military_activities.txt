Under most legal systems and even in relation to the laws of war such as might govern war crimes or acts prohibited even in relation to open belligerence and states of absolute war, such as the use of inhumane or prohibited weapons or techniques, the impersonation of a civilian in order to supposedly effect their rescue from a dangerous situation would be unlikely to be viewed as legally or procedurally acceptable, as well as being unwise from the standpoints of law enforcement considerations, judicial systems, military tactics, and governmental outlooks.
In nearly every case, disguise as a civilian, by a member of law enforcement or military member or official actor of any type (including employees or contractors of private enterprise acting in any sort of security related function which would essentially always relate to law enforcement or military roles, whether or not they were being performed by an actual member of such an organization and not someone appointed directly or by proxy because of their own organizational affiliation to act in that role) especially of a foreign population and even more extensively of a different species is likely to be viewed by each and every party that became aware of those circumstances or events to be a severe breach of peace and the rule of along and most likely considered along the lines of disuised invasion, which would be treated in the United States of America and other signatories to the Geneva Convention as participating in unlawful combat activities, and specifically lead to identification of any involved in such activities as unlawful combatants, subject to no consideration of due process as would exist as protection under legal systems, and instead being absolutely identified as an exigent threat to the place invaded, and would render any involved and therefore determinable to be unlawful combatants subject to summary execution.
