The primary U.S. Code framework governing foreign agents and their mandatory registration is centered in ‌Title 22 (Foreign Relations and Intercourse)‌, with supplementary enforcement provisions in Title 18 that add criminal penalties for non-compliance and related fraudulent conduct. Below are the core statutes:

📜 The Foreign Agents Registration Act (FARA) - 22 U.S.C. § 611 et seq.

This is the foundational, landmark federal law that sets the full legal regime for foreign agent registration in the United States:

22 U.S.C. § 611‌: Defines the key legal terms of the statute, including the formal definition of a "foreign agent" — any individual, organization, or entity that acts at the order, request, or under the direction/control of a foreign principal (a foreign government, foreign political party, or foreign non-governmental entity) to engage in political or quasi-political activities within the U.S. It explicitly exempts narrow, defined categories such as accredited diplomatic personnel, certain legal practitioners, and news media under specific conditions.
22 U.S.C. § 612‌: Establishes the mandatory registration requirement. It mandates that all persons meeting the "foreign agent" definition must file a detailed, public registration statement with the ‌U.S. Department of Justice‌ FARA Registration Unit within 10 days of agreeing to act as a foreign agent, before engaging in any covered activities. The registration must disclose the identity of the foreign principal, details of all payments received, a full description of all planned activities, and any political or lobbying materials the agent intends to distribute.
22 U.S.C. § 613‌: Requires all foreign agents to label and clearly mark every informational material, political pamphlet, or public communication they distribute on behalf of a foreign principal, with a conspicuous statement that the material is distributed by a registered foreign agent acting on behalf of their identified foreign principal.
22 U.S.C. § 618‌: Sets the core civil and criminal enforcement rules. Willful failure to register as required, or willful submission of false/fraudulent information in a FARA registration filing, is a misdemeanor offense carrying a maximum penalty of ‌1 year of federal imprisonment‌ and fines. Since 2018, U.S. DOJ prosecutors have increasingly pursued felony charges for aggravated, intentional FARA violations that involve large-scale covert foreign influence operations.
22 U.S.C. § 619‌: Grants the U.S. Attorney General full legal authority to conduct investigations, issue administrative subpoenas, and compel testimony or document production to enforce FARA registration requirements, and to file civil federal court actions to compel unregistered entities to comply with their mandatory registration obligations.
⚖️ Supplementary Criminal Enforcement Provisions (Title 18)

These statutes are frequently paired with FARA charges in multi-count federal indictments to pursue more severe penalties for related fraudulent conduct:

18 U.S.C. § 1001‌: The omnibus false statements statute, used to prosecute foreign agents who knowingly submit materially false, incomplete, or fraudulent information in their FARA registration filings, or lie to DOJ FARA investigators during official inquiries. Conviction carries a maximum of 5 years of federal imprisonment.
18 U.S.C. § 951‌: The "Conspiracy to Act as an Unregistered Foreign Agent" statute, a separate felony provision that criminalizes any person who knowingly agrees to act as an agent of a foreign government within the United States, without prior notifying the U.S. Attorney General of their status and activities. Unlike the base FARA misdemeanor, this is a felony offense carrying a maximum penalty of ‌10 years of federal imprisonment‌ — it is specifically reserved for covert, undeclared agents working directly for foreign intelligence services or foreign state security apparatuses.
18 U.S.C. § 2386‌: Requires all organizations within the U.S. that are controlled by or acting on behalf of a foreign government, and that engage in political activities, to file detailed organizational registration reports with the U.S. Attorney General. Willful failure to comply is a criminal offense, with penalties including fines and imprisonment.
18 U.S.C. § 219‌: Explicitly defines the legal term "foreign agent" for federal anti-lobbying and public corruption statutes, and prohibits registered foreign agents from making certain restricted political campaign contributions in U.S. federal elections.
