Building a cross‑domain anomaly “operating system”— Let’s start with Financial / Business / Economic related processes, then we can move to the next domain on the list.

## Financial flows anomaly workflow (specialized)

### 1. What “normal” looks like in financial flows

- **Baseline dimensions:**
  - **Volume:** Typical transaction amounts per account, per day/week/month.
  - **Frequency:** Normal transaction counts and rhythms (payroll cycles, rent, utilities).
  - **Counterparties:** Usual payers/payees, geographies, institutions.
  - **Channels:** Card, ACH, wire, cash deposits, crypto, checks.
  - **Narrative:** “For this entity, a normal month looks like: salary in, rent out, 3–10 card purchases, 1–2 transfers.”

---

## 2. Detection workflow (financial flows)

### 2.1 Automated signals

- **Thresholds:**
  - **Unusual amount:**  
    \[
    \text{z-score} > k \quad \text{for transaction amount vs. historical mean}
    \]
- **Velocity:**
  - **Bursting:** Many transactions in a short window, especially to new recipients.
- **Pattern shifts:**
  - Sudden change in **merchant category**, **country**, or **time of day**.
- **Structure anomalies:**
  - Round‑number transfers, repeated small amounts, daisy‑chain transfers between related accounts.

### 2.2 Human/LLM qualitative signals

- **Narrative mismatch:** Description or memo doesn’t fit the pattern (“consulting fee” from a known retail customer).
- **Context mismatch:** Transaction doesn’t fit known life/business events (e.g., large wire to high‑risk jurisdiction with no prior link).
- **Sequence mismatch:** Order of events is off (refund before purchase, salary after massive outgoing transfers).

## 3. Financial flows triage decision framework

```text
[Start: Suspicious transaction or pattern detected]

1. Is there immediate risk of irreversible loss or legal exposure?
   - Large outbound transfer?
   - High-risk jurisdiction or sanctioned entity?
   - Use of high-risk channels (crypto mixers, shell entities)?

        Yes ──> Immediate containment:
                - Freeze or hold transaction (if allowed)
                - Alert fraud/compliance team
                - Preserve logs and evidence
                END (for now)

        No ──> Continue

2. Is this anomaly isolated or part of a pattern?

   - Check last 30–90 days for:
     - Similar counterparties
     - Similar amounts
     - Similar timing or channels

        Pattern ──> Treat as potential scheme:
                    - Create case file
                    - Link related transactions
                    - Assign investigator

        Isolated ──> Treat as single-event anomaly:
                     - Log
                     - Monitor for recurrence

3. Does it conflict with known customer/entity profile?

   - Income level, business type, geography, historical behavior.

        Strong conflict ──> High-priority review
        Mild conflict   ──> Medium-priority; monitor
        No clear conflict ──> Low-priority; keep in anomaly log
```

## 4. Root-cause and scheme exploration (financial flows)

### 4.1 Hypothesis categories

- **Benign explanations:**
  - One‑off large purchase, life event (house, car, medical), business expansion.
- **Operational issues:**
  - Mis‑coded merchant category, batch posting, delayed settlement, duplicate entries.
- **Risky but non‑criminal:**
  - Tax optimization, asset protection, informal lending.
- **Criminal/abusive:**
  - Money laundering, fraud, embezzlement, sanctions evasion, terrorist financing.

### 4.2 Investigation steps

- **1. Data integrity check:**
  - Are timestamps, amounts, currencies, and counterparties recorded correctly?
- **2. Profile check:**
  - Does this fit any plausible life/business event for this entity?
- **3. Link analysis:**
  - Graph connections between accounts, counterparties, jurisdictions.
- **4. Temporal analysis:**
  - Look at **before/after** windows:
    - Inflows preceding outflows
    - Structuring (many small deposits → one large transfer)
- **5. External context:**
  - Sanctions lists, adverse media, known typologies (e.g., trade‑based money laundering patterns).

function llm_triage_transaction(tx, account_profile, history):

function llm_explore_scheme(anomaly_cluster, graph_context):

## 6. Codifying learning (financial flows)

### 6.1 Detection rules/playbooks

- **Rules examples:**
  - **Rule:** “If outbound wires > X times monthly average and to new foreign counterparties → flag high‑risk anomaly.”
  - **Rule:** “If many small cash deposits followed by a single large transfer within 48 hours → possible structuring.”
- **Playbook elements:**
  - **Trigger:** Which pattern activates the playbook.
  - **Immediate checks:** Verify identity, confirm transaction purpose, check sanctions/adverse media.
  - **Escalation path:** Who gets notified (fraud, compliance, law enforcement liaison).
  - **Evidence preservation:** Logs, screenshots, communication records.

### 6.2 Recurrence methodology

- **Cluster recurring anomalies** by:
  - Counterparty, jurisdiction, channel, amount band, time window.
- **Name the pattern** (“late‑night micro‑wires to offshore exchanges”).
- **Attach a typology** (if known) or mark as “emerging pattern.”
- **Update models and humans:**
  - Add labeled examples to training sets.
  - Update analyst training materials with “pattern cards.”
