Fifth Extension: The Collapse of Control Exclusivity
### *No actor controls the system trajectory in a multi‑actor environment*

```latex
\begin{lemma}[Collapse of Control Exclusivity]
\label{lemma:control}
Let $P$ be an event in the multi-actor environment described in
Theorem~\ref{thm:unified_exclusivity}, with observers
$\mathcal{O}$, interpreters $\mathcal{O}$, and responders
$\mathcal{A}$.

For each actor $X \in \mathcal{O} \cup \mathcal{A}$, let
$r_X \in R_X$ denote its response and let the system trajectory
$T$ be the (possibly stochastic) evolution of the environment after $P$,
determined by the joint action profile
\[
\mathbf{r} = (r_X)_{X \in \mathcal{O} \cup \mathcal{A}}.
\]

Suppose a focal actor $X_f$ believes it can unilaterally determine the
trajectory $T$ (``control exclusivity''). If there exists at least one
additional actor $X_k \neq X_f$ with a non-trivial action set $R_k$ and
a payoff function that depends on $P$ or $\{N_i\}$, then as the number
of such actors increases,
\[
\lim_{|\mathcal{O}\cup\mathcal{A}|\to\infty}
\Pr\big(T = T_{X_f} \text{ (the trajectory intended by } X_f)\big) = 0.
\]

Consequently, in multi-actor environments with overlapping observation,
heterogeneous interpretation, multiple responders, and divergent
expectations, no actor can monopolize the system trajectory. Equilibrium
strategies must treat system evolution as emergent, interactive, and
beyond unilateral control.
\end{lemma}
```

# **Interpretation: What this means in CI/HUMINT**

This is the most important extension for your Informant–Handler Game.

Once you accept:

- **many actors observe**,  
- **many actors interpret**,  
- **many actors respond**,  
- **many actors project futures**,  

then the final step is unavoidable:

> **No actor controls what happens next.**

This is the collapse of **control exclusivity**.

---

## **1. Handlers do not control the trajectory**

Handlers often believe:

- “If I manage the CI correctly, the case will go where I want.”  
- “If I shape the narrative, the operation will stay contained.”  
- “If I anticipate the offender’s next move, I can steer the outcome.”

But in reality:

- rival agencies act,  
- offenders adapt,  
- supervisors intervene,  
- prosecutors shift priorities,  
- media reframes the story,  
- public sentiment changes,  
- political actors interfere.

The handler’s intended trajectory \(T_{H}\) is only one trajectory among many.

---

## **2. CIs do not control the trajectory**

CIs often believe:

- “If I feed the handler the right story, I can steer the case.”  
- “If I exaggerate risk, I’ll get more protection.”  
- “If I shade the truth, I can manipulate the future.”

But:

- offenders react unpredictably,  
- rival criminals interfere,  
- other sources contradict them,  
- agencies cross‑check,  
- handlers reinterpret,  
- prosecutors reframe,  
- media leaks shift the environment.

The CI’s intended trajectory \(T_{S}\) is not the system trajectory.

---

## **3. Offenders do not control the trajectory**

Offenders believe:

- “If I lie low, the heat will die down.”  
- “If I intimidate the CI, the case collapses.”  
- “If I move fast, I can outpace enforcement.”

But:

- enforcement adapts,  
- other criminals exploit the vacuum,  
- media attention spikes,  
- agencies coordinate unexpectedly,  
- political pressure shifts priorities.

Their intended trajectory \(T_{O}\) collapses under multi‑actor interference.

---

## **4. Agencies do not control the trajectory**

Agencies believe:

- “We own this case.”  
- “We control the narrative.”  
- “We determine the outcome.”

But:

- rival agencies intervene,  
- prosecutors override,  
- media reframes,  
- public sentiment shifts,  
- political actors distort incentives,  
- offenders adapt,  
- CIs defect.

The system trajectory is emergent, not owned.

---

# **5. Why this matters for your unified model**

This fifth extension completes the architecture:

1. **Observation exclusivity collapses**  
   → many see.

2. **Interpretation exclusivity collapses**  
   → many mean.

3. **Response exclusivity collapses**  
   → many act.

4. **Expectation exclusivity collapses**  
   → many predict.

5. **Control exclusivity collapses**  
   → no one controls the trajectory.

This is the full multi‑actor environment:  
**a system where no actor can monopolize any layer of the information‑action cycle.**

Your Informant–Handler Game becomes a *local interaction* inside a global, uncontrollable system.
