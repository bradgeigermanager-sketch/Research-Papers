### Lemma: Collapse of observational exclusivity

Here’s the matching lemma, parallel to your “sole source of response” lemma, but on the **observation** side—ready to drop into your LaTeX model.

```latex
\begin{lemma}[Collapse of Observational Exclusivity in Multi-Actor Environments]
\label{lemma:observation}
Let an event or problem $P$ occur in an environment populated by a set
of potential observers $\mathcal{O} = \{O_1, O_2, \dots, O_n\}$, where
each $O_i$ has an independent, non-trivial observation channel
(e.g.\ sensors, informants, media access, technical collection).

Suppose a focal actor $O_f \in \mathcal{O}$ believes it is the sole
observer of $P$ and conditions its strategy on this belief
(``observational exclusivity''). If there exists at least one additional
observer $O_k \neq O_f$ such that
\[
\Pr(O_k \text{ observes } P) > 0,
\]
then the assumption of observational exclusivity by $O_f$ is
structurally unstable.

In particular, as the number of independent observers $|\mathcal{O}|$
with $\Pr(O_i \text{ observes } P) > 0$ increases, the probability that
$O_f$'s private observation remains unique converges to zero, and the
expected payoff to any strategy that relies on being the sole observer
(e.g.\ unchallenged narrative control, undetected exploitation) strictly
decreases.

Consequently, in any multi-actor environment with overlapping
observation channels, equilibrium strategies must treat observation as a
shared condition rather than an exclusive asset.
\end{lemma}
```

### Systems diagram: the Observer Graph

Below is a precise **diagram spec** you can turn into TikZ (or any graph tool) to represent the “Multiplicity of Eyes” idea.

#### 1. Node types

- **Event node \(P\):**  
  - Single central node.  
  - Label: `P` / “Event / Problem”.

- **Observer nodes \(O_i\):**  
  - Nodes arranged in a ring around \(P\).  
  - Types (encode via shape or color if you like):  
    - **State:** law enforcement, intel, regulators.  
    - **Criminal:** gangs, cartels, adversaries.  
    - **Private:** platforms, corporations, NGOs.  
    - **Public/Media:** journalists, crowd, bystanders.  
    - **Automated:** sensors, algorithms, OSINT scrapers.

- **Secondary nodes (optional):**  
  - **Narrative nodes \(N_i\):** each observer’s interpretation.  
  - **Leak nodes \(L_i\):** channels where observation escapes (media, courts, social media, foreign services).

#### 2. Edge semantics

- **Observation edges:**  
  - Directed edges \(P \rightarrow O_i\) or \(O_i \leftrightarrow P\).  
  - Meaning: “\(O_i\) can observe \(P\) via some channel.”

- **Interpretation edges:**  
  - \(O_i \rightarrow N_i\).  
  - Meaning: observer \(O_i\) generates its own narrative or analytic product.

- **Cross‑observer edges:**  
  - \(O_i \leftrightarrow O_j\).  
  - Meaning:  
    - information sharing,  
    - competition,  
    - mutual monitoring,  
    - or adversarial inference.

- **Leak / propagation edges:**  
  - \(O_i \rightarrow L_k\), \(L_k \rightarrow O_j\).  
  - Meaning: observation escapes the original observer’s control and reaches others.

#### 3. TikZ-style layout sketch

Conceptual layout you can implement:

- Place \(P\) at the center: `(0,0)`.  
- Place observers on a circle of radius `r`:

  \[
  O_i: (r \cos \theta_i,\ r \sin \theta_i),\quad \theta_i = \frac{2\pi i}{n}.
  \]

- Draw:

  - arrows from \(P\) to each \(O_i\);  
  - small narrative nodes \(N_i\) slightly offset from each \(O_i\);  
  - a few cross‑edges between selected \(O_i\) to show interaction.
