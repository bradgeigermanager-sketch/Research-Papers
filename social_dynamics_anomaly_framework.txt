Social dynamics anomalies aren’t about machines or paperwork.  
They’re about people, groups, narratives, emotions, incentives, and emergent behavior.  
They’re subtle, contextual, and often only visible when you know what “normal social reality” looks like.

Below is a **full anomaly‑detection + reaction framework** for social dynamics, built to the same standard as the other domains.

---

# **SOCIAL DYNAMICS ANOMALY FRAMEWORK**  
## *Detecting, interpreting, and responding to unusual group behavior, narrative shifts, and social-pattern disruptions*





---

# **1. What “normal” looks like in social systems**

You cannot detect anomalies without a baseline.  
In social dynamics, “normal” is defined across **four layers**:

## **1.1 Behavioral baselines**
- Typical interaction frequency  
- Usual alliances, rivalries, cliques  
- Expected cooperation/competition levels  
- Normal emotional tone (calm, tense, neutral)

## **1.2 Narrative baselines**
- Dominant stories people tell about themselves and others  
- Expected rumor velocity  
- Typical sources of information  
- Normal disagreement patterns

## **1.3 Structural baselines**
- Usual hierarchy or influence structure  
- Expected gatekeepers and brokers  
- Normal flow of information  
- Typical decision-making pathways

## **1.4 Environmental baselines**
- Stressors (economic, political, organizational)  
- Incentives and constraints  
- Cultural norms and taboos  

These baselines let you detect **behavioral**, **narrative**, **structural**, and **contextual** anomalies.

---

# **2. Detection workflow (social dynamics)**





## **2.1 Behavioral anomaly signals**
- Sudden alliance shifts  
- Unexpected cooperation between rivals  
- Abrupt withdrawal of a key actor  
- Increase in secrecy or side‑channel communication  
- New gatekeepers emerging

## **2.2 Narrative anomaly signals**
- Rumors spreading faster than usual  
- New narratives displacing old ones  
- Sudden consensus where disagreement is normal  
- Contradictions between public and private statements  
- “Too coordinated” messaging

## **2.3 Structural anomaly signals**
- New influencers rising quickly  
- Old influencers losing centrality  
- Information bottlenecks forming  
- Fragmentation of previously cohesive groups  
- Emergence of subgroups with distinct norms

## **2.4 Contextual anomaly signals**
- Behavior inconsistent with incentives  
- Emotional tone shifts (fear, anger, apathy)  
- Unusual risk-taking or risk-aversion  
- Abrupt changes in participation or engagement

# **3. Triage decision tree (social dynamics)**

```text
[Start: Social anomaly detected]

1. Does the anomaly indicate rising conflict or instability?
   - Escalating hostility?
   - Rapid rumor spread?
   - Group polarization?

        Yes → High-priority stabilization:
               - Increase monitoring
               - Identify conflict drivers
               - Engage trusted intermediaries

        No → Continue

2. Does the anomaly indicate hidden coordination or covert activity?
   - Sudden alignment of messaging?
   - Unusual secrecy?
   - Parallel communication channels?

        Yes → Investigate:
               - Map communication patterns
               - Identify brokers and influencers
               - Look for triggering events

        No → Continue

3. Does the anomaly indicate loss of trust or legitimacy?
   - Withdrawal from participation?
   - Avoidance of leadership?
   - Cynicism or disengagement?

        Yes → Trust-repair track:
               - Identify grievances
               - Increase transparency
               - Rebuild communication pathways

        No → Continue

4. Is the anomaly isolated or part of a pattern?
   - Compare across time, groups, contexts

        Pattern → Open social-dynamics case
                   - Build timeline
                   - Identify drivers
                   - Predict trajectories

        Isolated → Log and monitor
```

---

# **4. Investigation workflow (social dynamics)**





## **4.1 Behavioral investigation**
- Map interactions before/after anomaly  
- Identify who changed behavior first  
- Look for:
  - New alliances  
  - New conflicts  
  - New absences  
  - New gatekeepers  

## **4.2 Narrative investigation**
- Track rumor origin and propagation  
- Identify narrative entrepreneurs  
- Compare public vs private narratives  
- Look for:
  - Sudden message discipline  
  - Contradictions  
  - Emotional tone shifts  

## **4.3 Structural investigation**
- Run influence/centrality analysis  
- Detect subgroup formation  
- Identify new brokers or bridges  
- Look for:
  - Fragmentation  
  - Consolidation  
  - Shadow hierarchies  

## **4.4 Contextual investigation**
- Identify triggering events  
- Examine incentive changes  
- Look for:
  - Stressors  
  - Resource shifts  
  - External shocks

function llm_social_root_cause(anomaly_record, network_data):

# **6. Human cognitive checklists (social dynamics)**

## **6.1 “Behavior feels off” checklist**
- Who changed behavior first?  
- Who followed?  
- Who benefits from the change?  
- What incentives shifted?  
- What emotions are present?  

## **6.2 “Narrative shift” checklist**
- What story is being told now?  
- Who is telling it?  
- Who is amplifying it?  
- What story did it replace?  
- What emotions does it evoke?  

## **6.3 “Group instability” checklist**
- Are subgroups forming?  
- Are bridges breaking?  
- Are leaders losing influence?  
- Are norms weakening?  
- Are conflicts escalating?

# **7. Codifying learning (social dynamics)**

## **7.1 Detection rules**
- “If rumor velocity exceeds baseline by X → flag narrative anomaly.”  
- “If centrality of a new actor rises > Y% in Z days → flag structural shift.”  
- “If emotional tone shifts sharply → flag contextual anomaly.”  

## **7.2 Playbooks**
- **Trigger:** Sudden alliance shift  
  - **Immediate checks:**  
    - Identify triggering event  
    - Map new alliances  
    - Assess conflict risk  
  - **Stabilization:**  
    - Engage trusted intermediaries  
    - Increase transparency  
    - Rebuild communication channels  

## **7.3 Recurrence methodology**
- Cluster anomalies by:
  - Group  
  - Narrative  
  - Trigger  
  - Emotional tone  
- Build **pattern cards**:
  - Signature  
  - Drivers  
  - Risks  
  - Stabilizers
