### Unified model for multi‑actor environments

Here’s a single, coherent formal model that integrates **observation**, **interpretation**, and **response** into one multi‑actor framework, with a unified “collapse of exclusivity” result. You can drop this straight into your LaTeX paper as a new section.

---

#### 1. Environment and actors

Let a problem or event be denoted by \(P\).

There are three key role‑sets:

- **Observers:** \(\mathcal{O} = \{O_1,\dots,O_n\}\)  
- **Interpreters:** typically the same set, each observer also interprets  
- **Responders:** \(\mathcal{A} = \{A_1,\dots,A_m\}\)

In many applications, \(\mathcal{O}\) and \(\mathcal{A}\) overlap (e.g., an agency both observes and responds).

Each observer \(O_i\) has:

- an information set \(I_i\) (what it can see or infer about \(P\)),  
- internal parameters \(\theta_i\) (priors, incentives, constraints),  
- an interpretive map
  \[
  N_i = f_i(P, I_i, \theta_i),
  \]
  where \(N_i\) is the narrative/interpretation produced by \(O_i\).

Each responder \(A_j\) has:

- access to some subset of interpretations \(\{N_i\}\),  
- an action set \(R_j\) (possible responses),  
- a payoff function \(U_{A_j}(r_j, P, \{N_i\}, \phi_j)\) with internal parameters \(\phi_j\) (mandate, pressure, risk tolerance).

---

#### 2. Three layers: observation, interpretation, response

1. **Observation layer**

   For each \(O_i\), define an observation event:
   \[
   \mathsf{Obs}_i(P) = 1 \quad \text{if } O_i \text{ observes } P,\quad 0 \text{ otherwise}.
   \]
   Assume
   \[
   \Pr(\mathsf{Obs}_i(P) = 1) > 0
   \]
   for multiple \(i\), reflecting overlapping observation channels.

2. **Interpretation layer**

   Conditional on \(\mathsf{Obs}_i(P) = 1\), observer \(O_i\) generates
   \[
   N_i = f_i(P, I_i, \theta_i),
   \]
   with heterogeneity:
   \[
   \exists i \neq k \text{ such that } f_i \neq f_k.
   \]

3. **Response layer**

   Each responder \(A_j\) chooses an action
   \[
   r_j \in R_j
   \]
   based on its accessible interpretations (its own and others’), e.g.
   \[
   r_j = \sigma_j\big(P, \{N_i : i \in \mathcal{I}_j\}, \phi_j\big),
   \]
   where \(\mathcal{I}_j\) is the set of interpretations available to \(A_j\).

---

#### 3. Unified collapse of exclusivity

You can state the unified result as a single theorem that subsumes the three lemmas.

```latex
\begin{theorem}[Unified Collapse of Exclusivity in Multi-Actor Environments]
\label{thm:unified_exclusivity}
Let $P$ be an event occurring in an environment with a set of observers
$\mathcal{O} = \{O_1,\dots,O_n\}$ and responders
$\mathcal{A} = \{A_1,\dots,A_m\}$ as defined above.

Assume:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item (Overlapping Observation) There exist at least two observers
  $O_i, O_k \in \mathcal{O}$ such that
  \[
  \Pr(\mathsf{Obs}_i(P) = 1) > 0
  \quad\text{and}\quad
  \Pr(\mathsf{Obs}_k(P) = 1) > 0.
  \]

  \item (Heterogeneous Interpretation) There exist $i \neq k$ with
  $f_i \neq f_k$, so that conditional on observation,
  \[
  \Pr(N_i \neq N_k \mid \mathsf{Obs}_i(P) = \mathsf{Obs}_k(P) = 1) > 0.
  \]

  \item (Multiple Responders) There exist at least two responders
  $A_j, A_\ell \in \mathcal{A}$ with non-trivial action sets
  $R_j, R_\ell$ and payoff functions that depend (directly or
  indirectly) on $P$ and $\{N_i\}$.
\end{enumerate}

Then, in the limit as the number of observers and responders with
non-zero observation probabilities and heterogeneous interpretive maps
increases:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(a)] \textbf{No observational exclusivity:} The probability that
  any single observer $O_f$ is the sole observer of $P$ converges to
  zero.

  \item[(b)] \textbf{No interpretive exclusivity:} The probability that
  any single narrative $N_f$ remains uncontested converges to zero.

  \item[(c)] \textbf{No response exclusivity:} The probability that any
  single responder $A_f$ is the sole source of response to $P$
  converges to zero.
\end{enumerate}

Consequently, any strategy by an actor that relies on (i) being the only
observer of $P$, (ii) monopolizing the interpretation of $P$, or
(iii) being the sole source of response to $P$ is structurally unstable
in such multi-actor environments. Equilibrium strategies must treat
observation, interpretation, and response as shared, contested, and
interdependent rather than exclusive.
\end{theorem}
```

---

#### 4. How this plugs into your existing game

- Your **Informant–Handler Game** sits inside this environment as one responder–observer pair \((S,H)\) embedded in a larger \((\mathcal{O},\mathcal{A})\) field.  
- The three earlier lemmas (observation, interpretation, response) become corollaries of Theorem \(\ref{thm:unified_exclusivity}\) under appropriate restrictions (e.g., fixing one layer and varying another).
