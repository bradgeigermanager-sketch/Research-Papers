### 1. Core game structure

**Players:**  
- **Handler:** law enforcement officer, case agent, or intelligence case officer.  
- **Informant/Source:** criminal, asset, or recruited spy with private information and private pressures (desperation, debt, ego, ideology).

**Stages (repeated game):**  
1. **Type realization (private to informant):**  
   - True‑intel, partial‑intel, or no‑intel.  
   - Desperation level: low, moderate, acute (addiction, debt, flight).  
2. **Signal choice (informant):**  
   - **Truthful report** (accurate, verifiable).  
   - **Strategic distortion** (mix of truth and spin).  
   - **Fabrication** (plausible but false).  
   - **Silence/withholding.**  
3. **Screening and response (handler):**  
   - **Accept and act** (operationalize, pay, grant leniency).  
   - **Probe** (delay, demand corroboration, test).  
   - **Discount** (log but don’t act).  
   - **Sanction** (cut off, charge, expose).  
4. **Outcome:**  
   - Operational result (success, neutral, failure, scandal).  
   - Update of beliefs about informant type and reliability.  
   - Update of informant beliefs about handler’s gullibility, tolerance for deceit, and reward function.

This is a **repeated, incomplete‑information game** with strong **reputation effects** on both sides.

---

### 2. Informant strategy space

From the informant’s perspective, the game is about **converting private narrative into resources or protection** under constraints.

**Key private variables:**

- **Desperation \(D\):** increases marginal utility of immediate reward and discounts future punishment.  
- **True information stock \(I\):** how much real, actionable intel they actually have.  
- **Perceived handler verification strength \(V\):** how likely lies will be tested and punished.  
- **Perceived handler hunger \(H\):** pressure for stats, wins, or “product.”

**Best‑response logic (informant):**

- When \(I\) high, \(D\) low, \(V\) high → **truthful or mildly spun reporting** dominates: reputation is valuable, long horizon.  
- When \(I\) low, \(D\) high, \(H\) high, \(V\) low → **fabrication** becomes attractive: short horizon, handler is “thirsty,” verification weak.  
- When \(I\) moderate, \(D\) moderate, \(V\) moderate → **strategic distortion**: mix truth with targeted lies (to harm rivals, steer operations).

Desperation effectively **tilts the payoff function**:  
- Immediate cash / leniency gets huge positive weight.  
- Future sanctions (being burned, prosecuted, killed) are heavily discounted.

---

### 3. Handler strategy space

The handler’s problem is a **screening and incentive‑design game** under institutional pressure.

**Key handler variables:**

- **Institutional pressure \(P\):** need for arrests, seizures, or “intelligence product.”  
- **Verification capacity \(V\):** surveillance, technical collection, cross‑sources, analytic support.  
- **Sanction credibility \(S\):** ability and willingness to punish deception.  
- **Source portfolio \(N\):** number and diversity of informants/assets.

**Handler choices:**

1. **Contract design:**  
   - Payment per tip vs per verified result.  
   - Leniency contingent on **pattern** of reliability vs single big “home run.”  
   - Use of **non‑monetary rewards** (status, access, ego‑stroking) to reduce cash‑driven fabrication.

2. **Screening rules:**  
   - Thresholds for acting on a tip (number of independent corroborations, technical confirmation).  
   - Differential scrutiny based on **source history** (Bayesian updating of reliability).  
   - Randomized “spot checks” and **sting‑tests** (feeding false info to see if it’s resold).

3. **Sanction policy:**  
   - Soft: quietly downgrade, stop paying, reduce access.  
   - Hard: prosecution, exposure, or in intel context, **blacklisting across agencies**.

**Best‑response logic (handler):**

- High \(P\), low \(V\), low \(S\) → handler is tempted to **over‑accept** and under‑screen, inviting fabrication.  
- High \(V\), credible \(S\), moderate \(P\) → handler can sustain a **high‑truth equilibrium**: informants learn that lies are detected and punished.  
- Over‑harsh \(S\) with noisy \(V\) → risk of punishing honest but unlucky sources, which can collapse cooperation.

### 4. Equilibrium patterns in law enforcement vs intelligence

#### 4.1 Law enforcement CI system

**Structure:**

- **Payoffs:** arrests, seizures, cleared cases, plea leverage.  
- **Verification:** controlled buys, surveillance, warrants, lab tests.  
- **Time horizon:** often **case‑bounded** (months–years), but informant may cycle across cases.

**Typical equilibria:**

- **“Stats‑driven lax screening” equilibrium:**  
  - High \(P\) for numbers, limited analytic capacity.  
  - Handlers overweight spectacular tips; informants learn that **dramatic narratives** get paid.  
  - Result: more **fabricated conspiracies**, staged controlled buys, and weaponized false tips against rivals.

- **“Professionalized CI management” equilibrium:**  
  - Formal source files, reliability scoring, mandatory corroboration.  
  - Payments tied to **verified outcomes**, not just information volume.  
  - Fabrication becomes less profitable; informants with low \(I\) either exit or shift to low‑stakes lies.

#### 4.2 Military / intelligence agency (spies, HUMINT)

**Structure:**

- **Payoffs:** strategic insight, early warning, access to denied areas, influence.  
- **Verification:** multi‑INT fusion (SIGINT, IMINT, OSINT), long‑term pattern analysis.  
- **Time horizon:** often **multi‑year or multi‑decade** relationships.

**Distinctive features:**

- **Higher stakes per error:** acting on bad intel can cause war, coups, or strategic failure.  
- **More complex type space:** ideological, coerced, double/triple agents, deception operations.  
- **Adversarial counter‑intelligence:** the “environment” is an active player trying to **feed you controlled sources**.

**Typical equilibria:**

- **“Access‑over‑rigor” equilibrium:**  
  - Leadership overvalues unique access; case officers become **advocates** for their source.  
  - Verification is politically or operationally constrained.  
  - Result: long‑running **fabricators** or controlled agents (e.g., double agents) shape policy.

- **“Counter‑deception‑aware” equilibrium:**  
  - Strong CI culture, red‑teaming, and structured analytic techniques.  
  - Sources are evaluated as **hypotheses**, not trusted persons.  
  - Incentives: rewards for **internal skepticism** and disconfirming evidence, not just “scoops.”  
  - Fabrication persists but is more likely to be **contained** and used as a signal about adversary intent.

### 5. Desperation and fabrication as a perturbation

You can think of **desperation** as a shock that temporarily changes the informant’s utility function:

\[
U_{\text{informant}} = \alpha \cdot \text{ImmediateReward} - \beta \cdot \text{FutureSanction} + \gamma \cdot \text{NonmaterialBenefits}
\]

- In normal conditions: \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) comparable; future matters.  
- Under acute desperation: \(\alpha \uparrow\uparrow\), \(\beta \downarrow\) (future heavily discounted).  

This pushes the informant into **risk‑seeking fabrication**, especially if:

- They believe handler’s \(V\) is low or can be gamed.  
- They observe other informants **getting away with** embellished or false intel.  
- The handler’s environment rewards **speed and drama** over accuracy.

On the handler side, desperation is often **unobservable**, but its effects are observable as:

- Sudden increase in volume and drama of tips.  
- Shift from granular, verifiable details to **grand narratives**.  
- More frequent requests for **up‑front payment** or immediate legal favors.

A rational handler in a repeated game should treat these as **state‑change signals** and temporarily:

- Raise verification thresholds.  
- Shift rewards from “big single tips” to **small, repeatedly verified contributions**.  
- Increase **non‑cash support** (treatment, relocation, structured debt‑relief via programs) to reduce \(\alpha\).

---

### 6. Design levers to move the equilibrium

If you wanted to **engineer** a safer equilibrium in both domains, the game‑theoretic levers are:

- **Contract form:**  
  - Pay for **verified, incremental truth**, not for spectacular, unverified claims.  
  - Build in **delayed rewards** contingent on long‑term reliability.

- **Belief updating and transparency (internally):**  
  - Formal reliability scores shared across units/agencies.  
  - Mandatory documentation of **failed tips** and their consequences.

- **Institutional incentives:**  
  - Reward handlers for **avoiding false positives**, not just for producing operations.  
  - Create career credit for **disconfirming** or downgrading bad sources.

- **CI and analytic integration:**  
  - Treat every source as a potential **deception channel**; model adversary incentives.  
  - Use structured analytic techniques (hypothesis testing, competing hypotheses) to reduce over‑reliance on any single informant.
