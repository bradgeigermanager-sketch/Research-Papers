### Lemma: Collapse of Concealment Exclusivity

```latex
\begin{lemma}[Collapse of Concealment Exclusivity]
\label{lemma:concealment}
Let $P$ be an event or fact in the multi-actor environment described in
Theorem~\ref{thm:five_collapses}, and let $\mathcal{X}$ denote the set
of actors (observers, interpreters, responders, forecasters, controllers).

For each actor $X \in \mathcal{X}$, let
\[
\mathsf{Know}_X(P,t) \in \{0,1\}
\]
indicate whether $X$ knows (or has correctly inferred) $P$ by time $t$,
and let $\mathsf{Reveal}_X(P,t) \in \{0,1\}$ indicate whether $X$ has
taken an action by time $t$ that makes $P$ accessible to at least one
additional actor (including institutions, media, or automated systems).

Assume:
\begin{itemize}
  \item[(i)] At least two actors have non-zero probability of eventually
  observing or inferring $P$ (directly or indirectly).
  \item[(ii)] At least one actor has a non-zero probability of
  $\mathsf{Reveal}_X(P,t) = 1$ conditional on knowing $P$.
  \item[(iii)] The number of actors with potential access to $P$ grows
  over time (through diffusion, inference, or institutional processes).
\end{itemize}

Let $\mathsf{Concealed}(P,t)$ denote the event that $P$ remains confined
to its initial holder(s) and has not been revealed to any additional
actor by time $t$. Then, under assumptions (i)–(iii),
\[
\lim_{t \to \infty}
\Pr\big(\mathsf{Concealed}(P,t)\big) = 0.
\]

Equivalently, in a sufficiently populated multi-actor environment with
diffusing access and non-zero reveal incentives, the probability that
$P$ remains permanently concealed converges to zero. Concealment is a
transient state, not a stable equilibrium.
\end{lemma}
```

### Control‑Concealment Graph  
#### How concealment decays under multi‑actor pressure

The Control‑Concealment Graph overlays **attempted control** with **concealment dynamics** to show why secrets erode over time in a multi‑actor field.

#### 1. Node types

- **Event/fact node \(P\):**  
  The concealed item: an action, relationship, failure, fabrication, or operation.

- **Actor nodes \(X\):**  
  Handlers, CIs, offenders, rival agencies, supervisors, prosecutors, media, oversight, etc.

- **Knowledge nodes \(K_X\):**  
  For each actor \(X\),
  \[
  K_X = \text{“X knows or has correctly inferred } P\text{”}.
  \]

- **Concealment‑attempt nodes \(H_X\):**  
  Actions by \(X\) intended to keep \(P\) hidden (withholding, classification, intimidation, compartmentation).

- **Reveal‑attempt nodes \(R_X\):**  
  Actions by \(X\) that increase the accessibility of \(P\) (leaks, testimony, disclosure, logging, investigative reporting).

- **Exposure node \(E\):**  
  The state in which \(P\) is accessible to a broad audience (institutional, public, or cross‑agency).

#### 2. Edge types

- **Knowledge edges:**
  - \(P \rightarrow K_X\): \(X\) learns or infers \(P\).
  - \(K_X \rightarrow H_X\): knowledge drives concealment attempts.
  - \(K_X \rightarrow R_X\): knowledge drives reveal attempts.

- **Concealment edges:**
  - \(H_X \rightarrow P\): attempts to keep \(P\) confined (compartmentation, suppression).
  - \(H_X \dashv R_Y\): concealment attempts interfere with others’ reveal attempts.

- **Reveal edges:**
  - \(R_X \rightarrow K_Y\): disclosure spreads knowledge to new actors.
  - \(R_X \rightarrow E\): large or public reveal (media, court, oversight).

- **Control edges:**
  - \(C_X \rightarrow H_X\) and \(C_X \rightarrow R_X\):  
    control strategies include both hiding and exposing, depending on incentives.

#### 3. Structural patterns

- **Concealment cluster:**  
  Nodes \(H_X\) around \(P\), trying to keep knowledge localized.

- **Reveal cluster:**  
  Nodes \(R_X\) pushing toward \(E\), often from actors with misaligned incentives (disgruntled insiders, rival agencies, media, oversight).

- **Diffusion pattern:**  
  As more \(K_X\) nodes light up over time, the number of potential \(R_X\) nodes grows, increasing pressure toward \(E\).

- **Decay of concealment:**  
  The longer the time horizon and the larger \(|\mathcal{X}|\), the more paths exist from \(P\) to \(E\) via some sequence
  \[
  P \rightarrow K_X \rightarrow R_X \rightarrow E.
  \]

#### 4. Layout sketch

- Center: \(P\).  
- Inner ring: \(K_X\) nodes (who knows).  
- One side: \(H_X\) nodes (who is trying to hide).  
- Other side: \(R_X\) nodes (who is trying or incentivized to reveal).  
- Top: exposure node \(E\).  
- Edges:
  - \(P \rightarrow K_X\), \(K_X \rightarrow H_X\) / \(R_X\),  
  - \(R_X \rightarrow K_Y\), \(R_X \rightarrow E\),  
  - \(H_X \dashv R_Y\) (interference).

Visually, as more \(K_X\) nodes appear and more \(R_X\) nodes activate, the graph becomes increasingly connected to \(E\), embodying the lemma: **the probability of permanent concealment collapses over time in a multi‑actor system.**
