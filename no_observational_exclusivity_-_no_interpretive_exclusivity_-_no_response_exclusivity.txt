Here are the three corollaries, written explicitly and ready to drop under the unified theorem.

```latex
\begin{corollary}[No Observational Exclusivity]
\label{cor:observation}
Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:unified_exclusivity}, fix any
focal observer $O_f \in \mathcal{O}$. If there exists at least one
additional observer $O_k \neq O_f$ with
\[
\Pr(\mathsf{Obs}_k(P) = 1) > 0,
\]
then as the number of observers with non-zero observation probability
increases, the probability that $O_f$ is the sole observer of $P$
converges to zero:
\[
\lim_{|\mathcal{O}|\to\infty}
\Pr\big(\mathsf{Obs}_f(P) = 1 \ \wedge\ 
\mathsf{Obs}_k(P) = 0 \ \forall k \neq f\big) = 0.
\]
Any strategy by $O_f$ that relies on being the only observer of $P$ is
therefore structurally unstable in such environments.
\end{corollary}
```

```latex
\begin{corollary}[No Interpretive Exclusivity]
\label{cor:interpretation}
Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:unified_exclusivity}, suppose
there exist observers $O_i, O_k \in \mathcal{O}$ with heterogeneous
interpretive maps $f_i \neq f_k$ and non-zero probability of joint
observation of $P$. Then the probability that any single narrative
$N_f$ remains uncontested converges to zero as the number of such
heterogeneous observers increases:
\[
\lim_{|\mathcal{O}|\to\infty}
\Pr\big(N_f \text{ is the unique interpretation of } P\big) = 0.
\]
Consequently, any strategy that relies on uncontested narrative control
over the meaning of $P$ is structurally unstable in multi-actor
environments with heterogeneous interpretive maps.
\end{corollary}
```

```latex
\begin{corollary}[No Response Exclusivity]
\label{cor:response}
Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:unified_exclusivity}, fix any
focal responder $A_f \in \mathcal{A}$. If there exists at least one
additional responder $A_\ell \neq A_f$ with a non-trivial action set and
payoff function that depends on $P$ and $\{N_i\}$, then as the number of
such responders increases, the probability that $A_f$ is the sole source
of response to $P$ converges to zero:
\[
\lim_{|\mathcal{A}|\to\infty}
\Pr\big(A_f \text{ responds to } P \ \wedge\ 
A_\ell \text{ does not respond } \forall \ell \neq f\big) = 0.
\]
Any strategy by $A_f$ that relies on being the only responder to $P$ is
therefore structurally unstable in such multi-actor environments.
\end{corollary}
```
