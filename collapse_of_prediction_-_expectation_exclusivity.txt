### Fourth extension: Collapse of prediction/expectation exclusivity

Here’s the next logical piece in the chain, written to match your existing lemmas and theorem.

```latex
\begin{lemma}[Collapse of Predictive Exclusivity]
\label{lemma:prediction}
Let $P$ be an event in the multi-actor environment described in
Theorem~\ref{thm:unified_exclusivity}, with observers
$\mathcal{O} = \{O_1,\dots,O_n\}$ and responders
$\mathcal{A} = \{A_1,\dots,A_m\}$.

For each actor $X \in \mathcal{O} \cup \mathcal{A}$, let
$\Pi_X(P)$ denote $X$'s predictive map over outcomes or trajectories
associated with $P$, defined on its information set $I_X$ and internal
parameters $\psi_X$:
\[
\Pi_X(P) = g_X(P, I_X, \psi_X),
\]
where $g_X$ may differ across actors.

Suppose a focal actor $X_f$ believes it can monopolize the prediction of
$P$ (``predictive exclusivity''), in the sense that its forecast
$\Pi_{X_f}(P)$ will uniquely shape expectations and planning. If there
exists at least one additional actor $X_k \neq X_f$ with non-trivial
information $I_k$ and a distinct predictive map $g_k \neq g_f$, then as
the number of such actors increases,
\[
\lim_{|\mathcal{O}\cup\mathcal{A}|\to\infty}
\Pr\big(\Pi_{X_f}(P) \text{ is the unique or expectation-dominant
prediction of } P\big) = 0.
\]

Consequently, in multi-actor environments with overlapping observation,
heterogeneous interpretation, and multiple decision-makers, any strategy
that relies on exclusive control over expectations or forecasts about
$P$ is structurally unstable. Equilibrium strategies must treat
prediction as contested and endogenous to the broader system of actors,
not as a private, unilateral asset.
\end{lemma}
```
