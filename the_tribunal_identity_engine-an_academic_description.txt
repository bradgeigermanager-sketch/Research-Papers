The Tribunal Identity Engine: An Academic Description

## Abstract

This model examines a recurring interplanetary judicial process in which a historically famous tribunal—analogous to the Nuremberg Trials—is repeatedly reenacted across multiple worlds. In each iteration, participants assume the identities of historical persons, undergo legal proceedings, receive official documentation, and subsequently retain those identities after the proceedings conclude. Over generations, the tribunal evolves from a judicial institution into a mechanism for manufacturing apparently legitimate identities on an industrial scale.

The resulting system produces widespread identity contamination, genealogical distortion, species misclassification, and institutional corruption.

***

## 1. Foundational Mechanism

In these settings, the legitimacy of identity derives not from biological origin but from documentary recognition.

The critical insight exploited by participants is that:

> An officially recorded identity is often treated as more authoritative than direct observation, memory, ancestry, or physical reality.

Accordingly:

1. An individual assumes Identity A.
2. A tribunal recognizes Identity A.
3. Judicial records are created.
4. Archives preserve those records indefinitely.
5. Other institutions defer to the archives.

The individual then emerges possessing documentary support for Identity A even though they were never originally that person.

The process is repeated thousands or millions of times.

***

## 2. Repetition Across Worlds

The most unusual feature is the continual recreation of the same tribunal.

Instead of a single Nuremberg proceeding, there are:

* Nuremberg I
* Nuremberg II
* Nuremberg III
* Nuremberg XII,000
* Nuremberg XII,001

and so forth.

The same defendants appear repeatedly.

The same judges appear repeatedly.

The same witnesses appear repeatedly.

However, none are the original historical individuals.

Each role is occupied by a different participant.

The names become detached from the persons who historically possessed them.

Over time, the identities function much like reusable licenses.

***

## 3. Transition from Trial to Identity Marketplace

The fictional tribunal gradually ceases to operate as a court.

Instead, it becomes a market.

Participants seek:

* social rank,
* citizenship,
* property claims,
* inheritance rights,
* institutional access,
* historical prestige.

The objective is no longer adjudication.

The objective becomes acquisition of authenticated identity packages.

An identity package might contain:

* judicial records,
* educational credentials,
* family relationships,
* tax histories,
* military service histories,
* citizenship records,
* genealogical links.

The tribunal thereby becomes an identity factory.

***

## 4. Generational Expansion

The system expands beyond the original participants.

For example:

A person acquires the identity "Defendant X."

Their spouse receives documents identifying them as the spouse of Defendant X.

Their children become documented descendants of Defendant X.

Their grandchildren inherit the same designation.

Eventually entire family trees emerge from identities that originated as tribunal roles.

At this stage a fictional society possesses millions of individuals who are legally descended from historical persons who never actually had those descendants.

***

## 5. Effects on the Impersonated

The direct harm in this fictional model is not merely fraud.

It is displacement.

The original identity holder becomes drowned beneath copies.

Suppose one historical person existed.

Ten million individuals subsequently acquire documentary claims to that same identity lineage.

Institutions become unable to distinguish:

* authentic history,
* adopted identities,
* fabricated descendants.

The original person effectively disappears beneath administrative noise.

***

## 6. Species Misclassification

This idea becomes especially interesting in a science-fiction context.

Suppose multiple intelligent species coexist:

* humans,
* synthetic beings,
* uplifted animals,
* extraterrestrials,
* engineered organisms.

The tribunal records were originally designed for humans.

However, all participants receive documentation identifying them as human.

Consequently:

An alien assumes a human identity.

The tribunal confirms it.

The alien's descendants inherit human designation.

Administrative systems subsequently classify entire populations as human despite biological reality.

Over centuries the documentary classification supersedes biological fact.

The society eventually reaches a point where:

* "human" means legally documented human,
* not biologically human.

Species categories become bureaucratic constructs.

***

## 7. Institutional Consequences

The fictional consequences would be profound.

### Archival Collapse

Archives cease functioning as records of reality.

Instead they become records of previous records.

Historical verification becomes impossible.

### Genealogical Inflation

Millions claim descent from identical ancestors.

Family trees converge into absurd complexity.

### Statistical Corruption

Population records become meaningless.

Birth data no longer correspond to biological populations.

### Legal Circularity

Identity becomes self-referential.

An individual is recognized because documents exist.

The documents exist because the individual is recognized.

Thus legitimacy derives entirely from administrative repetition.

***

## 8. Philosophical Interpretation

From a philosophical perspective, the tribunal ceases to determine truth.

Instead it manufactures truth.

The institution demonstrates a principle commonly explored in sociology and speculative fiction:

> A sufficiently trusted bureaucracy can transform administrative assertions into perceived reality.

The system survives not because the identities are true but because every institution accepts the documents created by every other institution.

***

## Conclusion

Such a recurring interplanetary Nuremberg-like tribunal could plausibly function as a vast "identity fraud machine" without focusing on operational details. Its primary effects would be cultural and epistemological rather than merely criminal. Repeated judicial authentication of assumed identities would gradually sever identity from biological origin, historical continuity, and even species membership. Over generations, the result would be a civilization in which legal records outweigh physical reality, producing widespread impersonation, false ancestry, species misclassification, and the effective erasure of the original individuals whose identities became endlessly recycled.
