## **3. Analysis Notes**

1. **Deauthentication-related attacks**

   * The matrix explicitly covers **classical and substitution attacks**:

     * **User absent → data manipulation**
     * **User identity reused → telepresence & session abuse**
   * Mitigation relies on **ephemeral tokens, signed FSM events, and immediate session termination**.

2. **Telepresence-specific threats**

   * Telepresence pods are ephemeral, sandboxed, and bound to CTs to **prevent privilege escalation or cross-node impersonation**.
   * No telepresence session persists if the authentication state transitions to DEAUTH or SUBSTITUTED.

3. **Node-to-node communication threats**

   * Attacks across distributed nodes (spoof, tamper, replay) are mitigated via **mutual attestation, signed messages, ephemeral CTs, and encrypted transport**.

4. **Audit & Forensics**

   * Append-only logs with cryptographic verification prevent **repudiation and tampering**, ensuring **post-incident analysis** is reliable.

5. **Zero Trust Enforcement**

   * Every component assumes **no implicit trust**, requiring **continuous validation of identity, CT, and FSM state**.

## **6. Key Security Principles Enforced**

1. **Authentication-state transitions are authoritative** – all telepresence and downstream operations are subordinate.
2. **All ephemeral tokens are bound to state** – no session persists after deauth.
3. **Zero-trust policy is enforced everywhere** – no component is implicitly trusted.
4. **Full observability** – immutable logs enable real-time monitoring and forensic analysis.
5. **Fail-closed by default** – any validation failure results in immediate session termination or denial.
