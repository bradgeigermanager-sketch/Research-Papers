Bad actors actively seek to be underestimated for their technical skill and operational capacity because it delivers layered, low-risk advantages that let them operate freely, avoid consequences, and maximize the damage or gain they can extract — all while flying under the target's radar.

Core Operational Advantages
Eliminates targeted scrutiny‌: When you are written off as technically unskilled, defenders will not assign you high-risk access restrictions, deep behavioral audits, or dedicated threat-hunting resources. You avoid being flagged as a priority threat, and can move through systems or teams for months or even years without triggering alerts.
Normalizes anomalous behavior‌: Any small, odd technical misstep you make can be easily dismissed as a clumsy, innocent mistake from a "non-technical user," rather than a sign of malicious activity. This lets you quietly test exploits, move laterally through systems, or siphon data without anyone pausing to investigate.
Cuts off mandatory accountability‌: You can plausibly claim you "don't understand the tech well enough" to explain your work, so no one will press you to demonstrate your capabilities, review your processes, or audit your output. This lets you hide hidden tools, backdoors, or stolen data completely unexamined.
Strategic Payoffs
Creates catastrophic blind spots for the target‌: The entire team will systematically deprioritize defenses against you, skip critical security upgrades, and ignore obvious red flags — because they have already decided you lack the skill to do real harm. This makes their entire defensive posture fragile and easy to break through.
Minimizes retaliation risk‌: If you are eventually caught, a reputation for being technically incompetent can reduce the perceived severity of your actions. Teams may write off the incident as an "innocent mistake" rather than a deliberate, sophisticated attack, leading to lighter consequences and less aggressive counter-action against you.
Sets up devastating asymmetric strikes‌: When the target is fully complacent, you can unleash a high-skill, advanced attack that completely overwhelms their underprepared defenses. The shock factor of a "low-skill" actor pulling off a sophisticated exploit will shatter their situational awareness, making coordinated response far slower and far less effective.

This tactic overlaps heavily with the principles of ‌passive/隐形攻击‌ (passive-aggressive hidden aggression) documented in social engineering and insider threat research, where bad actors use apparent harmlessness to disarm their targets before acting .
