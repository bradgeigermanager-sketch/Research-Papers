Corruption, Coercive Neurotechnology, and the Fiction of “Non-Sentient Sentient-Like Proxies”: An Academic Analysis

## Abstract

In areas where advanced mind-control and sensory-replacement technologies have become integrated into military, law-enforcement, judicial, and penal institutions, opportunities arise for abuse by corrupt officials. Such abuses may involve the involuntary manipulation of cognition, perception, behavior, or bodily autonomy. To conceal these activities, corrupt actors may promote the claim that apparent sentient victims are not genuine sentient persons but rather remotely controlled bodies inhabited by non-sentient organisms or functioning as biological proxies. This paper-style analysis examines the nature of these corrupt conditions, the false narratives used to rationalize them, and the logical inconsistencies inherent in such claims.

***

## Introduction

Technologies capable of altering perception, replacing sensory input, or influencing cognition would possess extraordinary power. In legitimate settings, such technologies might be justified for defense, rehabilitation, medical treatment, or training purposes. However, the same capabilities could create unprecedented opportunities for abuse when oversight mechanisms fail.

A recurring characteristic of institutional corruption is the development of narratives that redefine victims in ways that reduce their perceived moral status. In the present types of scenarios, corrupt officials attempt to justify coercive actions by asserting that apparent sentient beings are not actually sentient persons but instead non-sentient animals or remotely controlled biological entities inhabiting sentient-like bodies.

Such claims serve a political and psychological function: if accepted, they would remove the moral and legal protections normally afforded to persons.

***

## Conditions Favoring Corruption

Several conditions contribute to the emergence of the described abuses.

### Concentration of Technological Power

When a small group controls technologies capable of influencing thought, perception, memory, or behavior, significant power asymmetries emerge. Corrupt actors may exploit these asymmetries because victims face difficulty proving manipulation has occurred.

### Information Monopolies

Sensory-replacement systems could theoretically enable authorities to control what individuals perceive. This would create opportunities to manipulate evidence, testimony, and public understanding of events.

### Institutional Self-Protection

Organizations accused of misconduct may be motivated to create alternative explanations for apparent victims. By redefining victims as non-persons or artificial entities, wrongdoing can be presented as acceptable experimentation, maintenance, or technological operation rather than abuse.

### Erosion of Independent Verification

Where independent observers lack access to the true nature of events, institutional narratives may become difficult to challenge. Corrupt officials may therefore benefit from secrecy, restricted records, and controlled information channels.

***

## The Claim of Non-Sentient Human-Like Bodies

The central falsehood in this scenario is the assertion that beings appearing indistinguishable from ordinary people are in fact non-sentient organisms or remotely controlled biological constructs.

This narrative may take several forms:

1. **The Biological Proxy Claim**
   * Sentient-like bodies are said to contain non-sentient organisms.
   * Apparent consciousness is attributed entirely to remote control systems.

2. **The Substitute Identity Claim**
   * Actual persons are alleged to have been replaced by biologically engineered non-sentient replicas.

3. **The Remote Operator Claim**
   * Every observed action is attributed to external control rather than to a conscious individual.

4. **The Denial of Subjectivity Claim**
   * Observable signs of awareness are dismissed as programmed responses rather than evidence of genuine experience.

These claims function primarily as mechanisms of moral disengagement.

***

## Moral and Political Functions of the Falsehood

### Justification of Abuse

If victims can be portrayed as lacking consciousness, corrupt actors can claim no genuine harm occurred.

### Elimination of Consent Requirements

Sentient beings possess interests, preferences, and rights. Non-sentient objects do not. Redefining persons as objects removes the need for consent.

### Obscuring Accountability

If no person is recognized as having been harmed, investigations become more difficult. Misconduct effectively disappears from official records.

### Psychological Protection for Perpetrators

Corrupt officials may find it easier to engage in harmful conduct when convinced—or when claiming to be convinced—that targets are not truly conscious individuals.

***

## Logical Inconsistencies

Several apparent inconsistencies arise within the corrupt narrative.

### The Problem of Behavioral Evidence

If a body consistently demonstrates:

* language comprehension,
* emotional responses,
* long-term memory,
* self-reflection,
* creativity,
* personal identity,

then the most straightforward explanation is the presence of a conscious mind.

Claiming that all such indicators are products of non-sentient control systems creates a significantly more complex explanation than recognizing personhood.

***

### The Selective Recognition Problem

Corrupt actors may simultaneously:

* treat an individual as sufficiently intelligent to obey orders,
* respond to arguments,
* recognize testimony,
* negotiate with them,

while also claiming that the same individual is not sentient.

These positions are difficult to reconcile.

If the being can understand rights, obligations, and social relationships, the claim of non-sentience becomes increasingly implausible.

***

### The Responsibility Paradox

A body that supposedly lacks consciousness may nevertheless be blamed, punished, rewarded, or monitored.

This creates a contradiction:

* If the entity truly lacks agency, punishment is irrational.
* If punishment is considered meaningful, agency is effectively being acknowledged.

The corrupt narrative often oscillates between these positions depending on what benefits authorities at a given moment.

***

### The Identity Contradiction

Corrupt institutions may claim simultaneously that:

1. A person does not really exist as a conscious individual.
2. That same person's actions, beliefs, or behavior justify institutional intervention.

If the individual's consciousness is denied, then invoking their intentions or responsibility becomes logically inconsistent.

***

### The Verification Problem

A further inconsistency concerns evidence.

If authorities possess sufficient knowledge to declare that certain individuals are non-sentient biological proxies, they should also possess objective evidence supporting that conclusion.

When such evidence cannot be independently verified, the claim becomes unfalsifiable and functions more as an ideological assertion than an empirical one.

***

## Social Consequences

If accepted, the corrupt doctrine would produce severe societal effects.

### Collapse of Personhood Standards

The distinction between person and non-person could become politically determined rather than evidence-based.

### Increased Vulnerability

Any individual could be stripped of protections simply by being reclassified as a non-sentient proxy.

### Institutionalized Abuse

Actions that would ordinarily be recognized as coercion or victimization could be reframed as routine operations involving allegedly non-sentient entities.

### Public Distrust

Citizens would lose confidence in institutions whose authority depends upon accurately recognizing and protecting persons.

***

## Conclusion

Within these setting, corrupt officials exploit advanced neurotechnologies not merely through direct coercion but through the creation of ideological narratives that deny the personhood of victims. The claim that sentient-like individuals are actually non-sentient organisms or remotely controlled biological proxies serves as a mechanism for avoiding moral responsibility, legal accountability, and public scrutiny.

From a logical perspective, the situations contain numerous contradictions. Those attempts deny sentience while simultaneously relying on evidence of intelligence, agency, memory, communication, and social participation. As a result, the doctrine functions less as a coherent explanation of reality and more as a rationalization designed to legitimize abuse and conceal corruption. In academic terms, it can be understood as a form of institutional depersonalization in which disputed claims about the nature of consciousness are employed to weaken protections normally afforded to persons.
