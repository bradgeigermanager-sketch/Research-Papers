### 1. LaTeX‑ready model section

```latex
\section{Model: Informant--Handler Game}

We model the interaction between a handler (law enforcement CI handler or intelligence case officer) and a human source (criminal informant or HUMINT asset) as a repeated Bayesian game with incomplete information and reputation.

\subsection{Players, types, and beliefs}

There are two players:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Handler $H$.
  \item Source $S$.
\end{itemize}

The source has type
\[
  \theta_S = (I, M, D),
\]
where:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $I \in \{\text{high}, \text{medium}, \text{low}\}$ is the source's stock of genuine, actionable information.
  \item $M \in \{\text{cooperative}, \text{opportunistic}, \text{hostile/deceptive}\}$ is the source's strategic orientation.
  \item $D \in \{\text{low}, \text{moderate}, \text{acute}\}$ is the source's desperation level.
\end{itemize}
Only $S$ observes $\theta_S$; $H$ holds a prior $\mu_0(\theta_S)$.

The handler has type
\[
  \theta_H = (V, P, S_c),
\]
where:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $V \in \{\text{low}, \text{medium}, \text{high}\}$ is verification capacity (surveillance, technical collection, analytic support).
  \item $P \in \{\text{low}, \text{high}\}$ is institutional pressure for ``product'' (arrests, seizures, intelligence).
  \item $S_c \in \{\text{weak}, \text{strong}\}$ is the credibility of sanctions for deception.
\end{itemize}
Only $H$ observes $\theta_H$; $S$ holds a prior $\nu_0(\theta_H)$ based on experience and local culture.

\subsection{Stage game}

Time is discrete, $t = 1,2,\dots$. In each period:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Types $(\theta_S,\theta_H)$ are fixed and known only to their respective players.
  \item The source chooses a report $r_t \in R = \{T, SD, F, \varnothing\}$:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $T$: truthful report (within the limits of $I$).
    \item $SD$: strategic distortion (truth plus spin).
    \item $F$: fabrication (plausible but false).
    \item $\varnothing$: silence/withholding.
  \end{itemize}
  \item The handler observes a message $m_t$ induced by $r_t$ (content, claimed stakes), but not its truth.
  \item The handler chooses an action $a_t \in A = \{A, Q, D, K\}$:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $A$: act and reward (operationalize, pay, grant leniency).
    \item $Q$: probe (seek corroboration, delay).
    \item $D$: discount (log but do not act; minimal reward).
    \item $K$: sanction (cut off, prosecute, expose).
  \end{itemize}
  \item A world outcome $o_t \in O$ is realized (e.g.\ operational success/failure, collateral damage, scandal), which may partially reveal the truthfulness of $r_t$.
  \item Beliefs $\mu_{t+1}$ and $\nu_{t+1}$ are updated from the public history $h_t$ using Bayes' rule wherever possible.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Per-period payoffs}

The source's per-period utility is
\[
  u_S(r_t,a_t,o_t;\theta_S,\theta_H)
  = \alpha(D)\,R_t(r_t,a_t,o_t)
  - \beta(D)\,C_t(r_t,a_t,o_t)
  + \gamma\,N_t(r_t,a_t),
\]
where:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $R_t$ is the immediate reward (cash, leniency, protection).
  \item $C_t$ is the expected future cost (being burned, prosecuted, or killed).
  \item $N_t$ captures non-material benefits (status, revenge, ego).
  \item $\alpha(D)$ is increasing in desperation $D$ (raising the weight on immediate reward).
  \item $\beta(D)$ is decreasing in $D$ (future costs are discounted under desperation).
  \item $\gamma \ge 0$ is a constant weight on non-material benefits.
\end{itemize}

The handler's per-period utility is
\[
  u_H(r_t,a_t,o_t;\theta_H,\theta_S)
  = \delta(P)\,G_t(a_t,o_t)
  - \lambda\,L_t(a_t,o_t)
  - \kappa\,K_t(a_t),
\]
where:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $G_t$ is the operational gain (arrests, seizures, strategic insight).
  \item $L_t$ is the loss (failed operations, scandals, wasted resources).
  \item $K_t$ is the internal cost of screening and sanctioning.
  \item $\delta(P)$ is increasing in institutional pressure $P$ (overweighting visible gains).
  \item $\lambda,\kappa > 0$ are cost parameters.
\end{itemize}

Total utilities are discounted sums:
\[
  U_i = \sum_{t=1}^{\infty} \rho_i^{t-1} u_i(r_t,a_t,o_t;\theta_S,\theta_H),
  \quad i \in \{H,S\},
\]
with discount factors $\rho_H,\rho_S \in (0,1)$.

\subsection{Strategies and equilibrium}

A (behavioral) strategy for the source is a mapping
\[
  \sigma_S : (\theta_S,h_t) \mapsto r_t \in R,
\]
and for the handler
\[
  \sigma_H : (\theta_H,h_t,m_t) \mapsto a_t \in A.
\]

A \emph{Perfect Bayesian Equilibrium} (PBE) is a pair $(\sigma_S^*,\sigma_H^*)$ and belief system $\{\mu_t^*,\nu_t^*\}$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Given beliefs, $\sigma_S^*$ and $\sigma_H^*$ maximize the respective players' expected discounted utilities.
  \item Beliefs are updated from strategies and observed histories using Bayes' rule wherever possible.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Comparative statics}

The model highlights two key comparative statics:
\begin{itemize}
  \item As desperation $D$ increases, $\alpha(D)$ rises and $\beta(D)$ falls, making fabrication $F$ and aggressive strategic distortion $SD$ more attractive relative to truthful reporting $T$, holding $\theta_H$ fixed.
  \item As institutional pressure $P$ increases, $\delta(P)$ rises, making the handler more willing to act $A$ on thin evidence and less willing to probe $Q$ or discount $D$, thereby increasing the expected payoff to $F$ and $SD$ for the source.
\end{itemize}
High verification capacity $V$ and strong sanction credibility $S_c$ can sustain a ``high-truth'' equilibrium in which truthful reporting dominates fabrication for most source types.
```

### 2. Worked numerical examples

These are deliberately simple—enough to illustrate how desperation and institutional pressure flip best responses.

#### 2.1 Law enforcement CI example

Consider a single period with two source types:

- Type L: low desperation \(D_L\) → \(\alpha_L = 1\), \(\beta_L = 1\).  
- Type H: high desperation \(D_H\) → \(\alpha_H = 3\), \(\beta_H = 0.3\).

Assume:

- If the source tells the truth \(T\), the handler can verify with high probability and will pay \(R_T = 5\) and not sanction.  
- If the source fabricates \(F\), the handler:
  - In a **stats‑driven regime** (high \(P\), low \(V\)): pays \(R_F = 4\) with probability \(0.8\), detects and sanctions with probability \(0.2\).  
  - Sanction cost to the source if detected: \(C_F = 10\).

Compute expected utilities.

**Stats‑driven regime (high \(P\), low \(V\))**

For type L (low desperation):

- Truth:
  \[
  U_S^L(T) = \alpha_L \cdot 5 - \beta_L \cdot 0 = 5.
  \]
- Fabrication:
  \[
  \mathbb{E}[R_F] = 0.8 \cdot 4 + 0.2 \cdot 0 = 3.2,
  \]
  \[
  \mathbb{E}[C_F] = 0.2 \cdot 10 = 2.
  \]
  \[
  U_S^L(F) = \alpha_L \cdot 3.2 - \beta_L \cdot 2 = 3.2 - 2 = 1.2.
  \]
So type L prefers truth: \(T\) strictly dominates \(F\).

For type H (high desperation):

- Truth:
  \[
  U_S^H(T) = \alpha_H \cdot 5 - \beta_H \cdot 0 = 3 \cdot 5 = 15.
  \]
- Fabrication:
  \[
  U_S^H(F) = \alpha_H \cdot 3.2 - \beta_H \cdot 2
           = 3 \cdot 3.2 - 0.3 \cdot 2
           = 9.6 - 0.6 = 9.0.
  \]

Even here, truth still dominates—but only because we assumed the same reward for truth and fabrication is lower. Now change the handler’s behavior to reflect high pressure:

Suppose under high \(P\) the handler pays more for dramatic fabricated stories:

- Let \(R_F = 7\) (for “big conspiracy” narratives), same detection probability.

Then:

- For type H:
  \[
  \mathbb{E}[R_F] = 0.8 \cdot 7 = 5.6,\quad \mathbb{E}[C_F] = 2,
  \]
  \[
  U_S^H(F) = 3 \cdot 5.6 - 0.3 \cdot 2 = 16.8 - 0.6 = 16.2.
  \]

Now:

- \(U_S^H(F) = 16.2 > U_S^H(T) = 15\).

So:

- Low‑desperation sources still prefer truth.  
- High‑desperation sources now strictly prefer fabrication, because the handler’s stats‑driven reward schedule overpays for dramatic, weakly verified intel.

This is exactly the “desperation + institutional pressure” channel your paper describes.

If the agency instead ties payment to verified outcomes (e.g., \(R_F\) only paid when corroborated), the expected reward for \(F\) collapses and the inequality reverses.

#### 2.2 HUMINT / intelligence example

Now a stylized HUMINT case with two handler regimes:

- Regime A: access‑driven, weak CI (low \(V\), high \(P\)).  
- Regime B: counter‑deception‑aware (high \(V\), strong CI, moderate \(P\)).

Let a source with moderate desperation (\(\alpha = 2\), \(\beta = 0.7\)) choose between:

- \(T\): truthful reporting.  
- \(F\): systematic fabrication.

Assume:

- Truthful reporting yields a steady stipend \(R_T = 4\) per period and low sanction risk.  
- Fabrication yields:
  - In Regime A: high reward \(R_F = 8\) with detection probability \(p_A = 0.1\).  
  - In Regime B: moderate reward \(R_F = 5\) with detection probability \(p_B = 0.6\).  
- Sanction cost if detected: \(C = 20\).

Compute one‑period utilities.

**Regime A (access‑driven)**

- Truth:
  \[
  U_S(T) = 2 \cdot 4 - 0.7 \cdot 0 = 8.
  \]
- Fabrication:
  \[
  \mathbb{E}[R_F] = (1-p_A) \cdot 8 = 0.9 \cdot 8 = 7.2,
  \]
  \[
  \mathbb{E}[C_F] = p_A \cdot C = 0.1 \cdot 20 = 2,
  \]
  \[
  U_S(F) = 2 \cdot 7.2 - 0.7 \cdot 2 = 14.4 - 1.4 = 13.0.
  \]

Fabrication strictly dominates truth: the source is rewarded for spinning dramatic stories, and detection is rare.

**Regime B (counter‑deception)**

- Truth:
  \[
  U_S(T) = 2 \cdot 4 = 8.
  \]
- Fabrication:
  \[
  \mathbb{E}[R_F] = (1-p_B) \cdot 5 = 0.4 \cdot 5 = 2,
  \]
  \[
  \mathbb{E}[C_F] = p_B \cdot C = 0.6 \cdot 20 = 12,
  \]
  \[
  U_S(F) = 2 \cdot 2 - 0.7 \cdot 12 = 4 - 8.4 = -4.4.
  \]

Now truth strictly dominates fabrication. The same source type flips from “rational fabricator” in Regime A to “rational truth‑teller” in Regime B purely because the verification and sanction parameters changed.
