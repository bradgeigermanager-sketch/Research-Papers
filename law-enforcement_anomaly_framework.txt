Detection, Classification, and Response for Procedural Irregularities

# **1. What “normal” looks like in law‑enforcement processes**

To detect anomalies, you need a baseline across the major operational domains:

### **1.1 Case lifecycle baselines**
- Report → intake → assignment → investigation → charging → prosecution → closure  
- Expected **timelines**, **handoffs**, and **documentation** at each stage  
- Normal **case load** per officer/unit  
- Typical **clearance rates** and **investigative steps**

### **1.2 Evidence handling baselines**
- Chain‑of‑custody entries at each transfer  
- Expected storage locations  
- Standard logging intervals  
- Required signatures and timestamps

### **1.3 Officer activity baselines**
- Patrol routes  
- Shift timing  
- Report frequency  
- Use‑of‑force reporting patterns  
- Vehicle GPS logs  
- Body‑worn camera activation patterns

### **1.4 Administrative baselines**
- Warrants: issuance → service → return  
- Arrest documentation  
- Booking procedures  
- Court‑appearance notifications  
- Interagency communication logs

These baselines allow you to detect **temporal**, **procedural**, **behavioral**, and **documentation** anomalies.

# **2. Detection workflow (law‑enforcement specific)**

## **2.1 Automated anomaly signals**
- **Timeline deviations:**  
  - Case sits idle beyond expected window  
  - Evidence transfer missing expected timestamp  
  - Warrant served unusually fast/slow  
- **Documentation gaps:**  
  - Missing forms, missing signatures, missing BWC footage  
  - Incomplete arrest reports  
- **Behavioral anomalies:**  
  - Officer deviates from patrol route  
  - Sudden spike/drop in arrests, stops, or citations  
  - BWC turned off during critical moments  
- **Cross‑system mismatches:**  
  - CAD logs don’t match RMS entries  
  - GPS logs don’t match officer narratives  
  - Evidence room logs don’t match case file

## **2.2 Human/LLM qualitative signals**
- **Narrative contradictions**  
  - Officer statement conflicts with witness statement  
  - Report conflicts with video  
- **Sequence anomalies**  
  - Evidence logged before seizure  
  - Arrest time after booking time  
- **Context anomalies**  
  - Officer present at two locations simultaneously  
  - Case closed without required investigative steps  
- **Pattern anomalies**  
  - Repeated procedural shortcuts by same officer/unit  
  - Recurrent missing evidence in same chain of custody

# **3. Triage decision tree (law‑enforcement)**

```text
[Start: Procedural anomaly detected]

1. Does the anomaly pose immediate legal or safety risk?
   - Evidence integrity at risk?
   - Potential wrongful arrest/detention?
   - Officer safety issue?
   - Public safety issue?

        Yes → Immediate containment:
               - Secure evidence
               - Notify supervisor/IA
               - Freeze related actions
               - Document everything
               END (for now)

        No → Continue

2. Does the anomaly violate statutory or constitutional requirements?
   - Miranda issues?
   - Warrant defects?
   - Unlawful detention?
   - Improper search/seizure?

        Yes → High-priority legal review
               - Flag for prosecutor
               - Begin corrective action
               - Preserve all logs

        No → Continue

3. Is the anomaly isolated or part of a pattern?
   - Check last 6–24 months for:
     - Same officer
     - Same unit
     - Same evidence room
     - Same case type

        Pattern → Open administrative or IA case
                   Assign investigator
                   Expand scope

        Isolated → Log and monitor

4. Does the anomaly affect case outcome?
   - Evidence admissibility?
   - Witness reliability?
   - Procedural compliance?

        Yes → Case-level corrective plan
        No  → Administrative follow-up only
```

# **4. Root‑cause exploration (law‑enforcement)**

## **4.1 Hypothesis categories**

### **Benign explanations**
- Training gaps  
- System outages (CAD/RMS)  
- Misfiled paperwork  
- High workload causing delays  

### **Operational issues**
- Evidence room bottlenecks  
- Misconfigured GPS/BWC systems  
- Incorrect case routing  
- Court scheduling errors  

### **Risky but non‑malicious**
- Officer burnout  
- Informal shortcuts  
- Misunderstood policy  

### **Misconduct or criminality**
- Falsified reports  
- Evidence tampering  
- Unlawful stops/searches  
- Retaliatory arrests  
- Collusion with offenders

# **5. Investigation workflow (law‑enforcement)**

## **5.1 Evidence integrity investigation**
- Compare chain‑of‑custody logs with:
  - RMS entries  
  - Evidence room logs  
  - Officer narratives  
  - BWC timestamps  
- Look for:
  - Missing links  
  - Time gaps  
  - Unauthorized access  
  - Conflicting signatures  

## **5.2 Officer activity investigation**
- Cross‑check:
  - GPS logs  
  - CAD dispatch logs  
  - BWC activation logs  
  - Arrest/stop reports  
- Look for:
  - Impossible travel times  
  - Unlogged stops  
  - BWC deactivations during key events  

## **5.3 Case‑flow investigation**
- Compare case file to:
  - Required investigative steps  
  - Prosecutor expectations  
  - Statutory timelines  
- Look for:
  - Missing interviews  
  - Missing warrants  
  - Premature closure  
  - Unexplained delays

# **7. Human cognitive checklists (law‑enforcement)**

## **7.1 “Procedural anomaly” checklist**
- What *should* have happened at this stage?  
- What *actually* happened?  
- What documentation is missing or contradictory?  
- Who touched the case/evidence last?  
- Does this conflict with policy, law, or past practice?  
- Could this be:
  - A training issue?  
  - A system issue?  
  - A misconduct issue?  

## **7.2 “Officer activity anomaly” checklist**
- Does the officer’s narrative match:
  - GPS logs  
  - CAD logs  
  - BWC footage  
- Are there unexplained gaps?  
- Are there repeated patterns across shifts/days?  

## **7.3 “Evidence anomaly” checklist**
- Is chain‑of‑custody complete?  
- Are timestamps sequential and plausible?  
- Any unauthorized access?  
- Any mismatches between physical evidence and documentation?

# **8. Codifying learning (law‑enforcement)**

## **8.1 Detection rules**
- “If BWC is off during any use‑of‑force event → flag critical anomaly.”  
- “If evidence transfer lacks timestamp or signature → flag high‑risk anomaly.”  
- “If officer GPS contradicts narrative by > X miles → flag for review.”  

## **8.2 Playbooks**
- **Trigger:** Missing evidence log  
- **Immediate checks:**  
  - Verify physical evidence  
  - Check RMS/CAD logs  
  - Interview last custodian  
- **Escalation:**  
  - Supervisor → IA → prosecutor (if case‑impacting)  

## **8.3 Recurrence methodology**
- Cluster anomalies by:
  - Officer  
  - Unit  
  - Evidence room  
  - Case type  
  - Time period  
- Label patterns (“Recurring BWC deactivation cluster”)  
- Add to training and oversight dashboards
