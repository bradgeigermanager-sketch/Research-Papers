The Five Collapses Theorem
### *A unified structural result for multi‑actor environments*

```latex
\begin{theorem}[The Five Collapses Theorem]
\label{thm:five_collapses}
Let $P$ be an event occurring in a multi-actor environment populated by
observers, interpreters, responders, forecasters, and strategic actors
$\mathcal{X} = \mathcal{O} \cup \mathcal{A}$, where each actor $X \in \mathcal{X}$
possesses:

\begin{itemize}
  \item a non-trivial probability of observing $P$,
  \item a heterogeneous interpretive map $f_X$,
  \item a non-trivial action set $R_X$,
  \item a predictive map $\Pi_X$ over future trajectories,
  \item and a control-attempt map $C_X$ that pushes on the system trajectory.
\end{itemize}

Assume that at least two actors in $\mathcal{X}$ have non-zero
probability of observation, heterogeneous interpretive maps, non-trivial
action sets, divergent predictive maps, and distinct control attempts.

Then, as the number of such actors increases, the following five
probabilities all converge to zero:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(1)] \textbf{Observation Exclusivity:}
  \[
  \Pr\big(O_f \text{ is the sole observer of } P\big) \to 0.
  \]

  \item[(2)] \textbf{Interpretation Exclusivity:}
  \[
  \Pr\big(N_f \text{ is the uncontested interpretation of } P\big) \to 0.
  \]

  \item[(3)] \textbf{Response Exclusivity:}
  \[
  \Pr\big(A_f \text{ is the sole responder to } P\big) \to 0.
  \]

  \item[(4)] \textbf{Expectation Exclusivity:}
  \[
  \Pr\big(\Pi_{X_f}(P) \text{ is the dominant or unique forecast of } P\big) \to 0.
  \]

  \item[(5)] \textbf{Control Exclusivity:}
  \[
  \Pr\big(T = T_{X_f} \text{ (the trajectory intended by } X_f)\big) \to 0.
  \]
\end{enumerate}

Therefore, in any sufficiently populated multi-actor environment with
overlapping observation, heterogeneous interpretation, multiple
responders, divergent expectations, and competing control attempts, no
actor can monopolize any layer of the information-action cycle. The
system trajectory $T$ is emergent, interactive, and beyond unilateral
control.

Equilibrium strategies must treat observation, interpretation, response,
expectation, and control as shared, contested, and structurally
non-exclusive.
\end{theorem}
```

---

## **Interpretive Summary (for your prose section)**

The Five Collapses Theorem states that in any real-world CI/HUMINT or multi‑actor environment:

- **Many see.**  
- **Many interpret.**  
- **Many act.**  
- **Many predict.**  
- **Many try to steer the future.**

Because each layer is populated by multiple actors with different incentives, priors, and information sets, **no actor can monopolize any layer**.  
The system trajectory is the emergent result of **all** actors’ pushes, not the intention of any one.

This theorem is the mathematical backbone of your entire multi‑actor architecture.
