### 1. Formalizing the repeated Bayesian game

Let’s make the informal structure precise enough that you could drop it into a research paper or a technical appendix.

#### 1.1 Players and types

- **Players:**  
  - **Handler \(H\)** (law enforcement CI handler or intelligence case officer).  
  - **Source \(S\)** (criminal informant or HUMINT asset).

- **Source types \( \theta_S \):**  
  - **Information stock:** \(I \in \{\text{high}, \text{medium}, \text{low}\}\).  
  - **Moral/strategic orientation:** \(M \in \{\text{cooperative}, \text{opportunistic}, \text{hostile/deceptive}\}\).  
  - **Desperation:** \(D \in \{\text{low}, \text{moderate}, \text{acute}\}\).

  So \( \theta_S = (I, M, D) \). Only \(S\) observes \( \theta_S \); \(H\) has a prior \( \mu_0(\theta_S) \).

- **Handler types \( \theta_H \):**  
  - **Verification capacity:** \(V \in \{\text{low}, \text{medium}, \text{high}\}\).  
  - **Institutional pressure:** \(P \in \{\text{low}, \text{high}\}\).  
  - **Sanction credibility:** \(S_c \in \{\text{weak}, \text{strong}\}\).

  \( \theta_H = (V, P, S_c) \). In practice, \(S\) has a belief over \( \theta_H \) based on experience and local culture.

#### 1.2 Stage game (one period)

Each period \(t\) proceeds:

1. **Nature:** draws \( \theta_S \) and \( \theta_H \).  
2. **Source move:** \(S\) chooses a report strategy \(r_t \in \{T, SD, F, \varnothing\}\):  
   - \(T\): truthful report (within limits of \(I\)).  
   - \(SD\): strategic distortion (truth + spin).  
   - \(F\): fabrication (plausible but false).  
   - \(\varnothing\): silence/withholding.

3. **Handler observation:** \(H\) observes only the message \(m_t\) (content, style, claimed stakes), not its truth.

4. **Handler move:** \(H\) chooses \(a_t \in \{A, Q, D, K\}\):  
   - \(A\): act and reward (operationalize, pay/leniency).  
   - \(Q\): probe (seek corroboration, delay).  
   - \(D\): discount (log but don’t act, minimal reward).  
   - \(K\): sanction (cut off, prosecute, expose).

5. **World resolution:**  
   - If \(A\) or \(Q\) leads to an operation, the true state (whether the intel was accurate) is partially revealed via outcomes (success, failure, collateral damage, embarrassment).  
   - Both players update beliefs:  
     - \(H\): \( \mu_{t+1}(\theta_S | \text{history}) \).  
     - \(S\): beliefs about \( \theta_H \) and about the mapping from \(r_t\) to payoffs.

6. **Payoffs:**  
   - \(u_H(r_t, a_t; \theta_H, \theta_S)\): function of operational success, career risk, resource cost.  
   - \(u_S(r_t, a_t; \theta_S, \theta_H)\): function of immediate reward, future access, and sanction risk.

The full interaction is a **repeated game** with public history \(h_t\) (messages, actions, observed outcomes) and private types.

### 2. Payoff structure and desperation

We can write the source’s per‑period utility as:

\[
u_S = \alpha(D) \cdot R_t(r_t, a_t) - \beta(D) \cdot C_t(r_t, a_t) + \gamma \cdot N_t
\]

- \(R_t\): immediate reward (cash, leniency, protection).  
- \(C_t\): expected future cost (being burned, prosecuted, killed).  
- \(N_t\): non‑material benefits (status, ego, revenge on rivals).  
- \(\alpha(D)\): increasing in desperation.  
- \(\beta(D)\): decreasing in desperation (future is discounted).

Under **acute desperation**, \( \alpha \gg \beta \), so:

- Fabrication \(F\) becomes attractive whenever \(H\) is perceived as:  
  - high \(P\) (hungry for product),  
  - low \(V\) (weak verification),  
  - or low \(S_c\) (sanctions rarely applied).

For the handler, a simple payoff form:

\[
u_H = \delta(P) \cdot G_t(a_t, r_t) - \lambda \cdot L_t(a_t, r_t) - \kappa \cdot K_t(a_t)
\]

- \(G_t\): operational gain (arrests, seizures, strategic insight).  
- \(L_t\): losses (bad ops, scandals, wasted resources).  
- \(K_t\): internal cost of screening and sanctioning.  
- \(\delta(P)\): increasing in institutional pressure (overweights visible gains).

High \(P\) and low \(V\) push \(H\) toward **over‑accepting** and under‑screening, which in turn shifts \(S\)’s best response toward \(F\) and \(SD\).

### 3. Equilibrium sketches

#### 3.1 “High‑truth” equilibrium

Conditions:

- \(V\) high, \(S_c\) strong, \(P\) moderate.  
- Contracts: rewards tied to **verified outcomes** and **long‑term reliability**.  
- Sanctions: credible and occasionally used.

Equilibrium behavior:

- Most types with \(I \ge \text{medium}\) and \(M \neq \text{hostile}\) choose \(T\) or mild \(SD\).  
- Fabrication \(F\) is rare and quickly punished; fabricators exit or are removed.  
- Handler uses \(Q\) and \(D\) frequently; \(A\) is reserved for well‑corroborated cases.

This is the target for **professional CI management** and **mature HUMINT shops**.

#### 3.2 “Fabrication‑prone” equilibrium

Conditions:

- \(P\) high, \(V\) low, \(S_c\) weak.  
- Rewards: paid per tip or per “big story,” not per verified result.  
- Sanctions: rarely enforced; handlers themselves are rewarded for volume.

Equilibrium behavior:

- Desperate, low‑\(I\) sources choose \(F\) or aggressive \(SD\).  
- Handlers often choose \(A\) on thin evidence to meet metrics.  
- System accumulates **false positives**, wasted ops, and in intel context, vulnerability to **deception operations**.

This is the equilibrium your “Economics of Deceit” paper is essentially diagnosing.

### 4. Law enforcement vs intelligence instantiations

#### 4.1 Law enforcement CI game

- **State space:** local criminal markets, specific cases.  
- **Verification tools:** controlled buys, surveillance, warrants, lab tests.  
- **Time horizon:** case‑bounded but with repeated use of the same CI.

Typical differences:

- \(G_t\) is heavily tied to **arrest/seizure counts**; \(L_t\) often discounted unless there is a scandal.  
- \(V\) can be high at the tactical level (lab tests, controlled buys) but low at the **narrative level** (claims about conspiracies, rival culpability).  
- Desperation \(D\) is often driven by **addiction and debt**, making \(\alpha(D)\) very high.

Result:  
- Good at catching **fabricated drugs** (lab will fail), but vulnerable to **fabricated narratives** that redirect enforcement against rivals or innocents.

#### 4.2 Intelligence / HUMINT game

- **State space:** strategic intentions, military plans, political dynamics.  
- **Verification tools:** multi‑INT fusion, long‑term pattern analysis, CI.  
- **Time horizon:** multi‑year; sources can shape doctrine and policy.

Differences:

- \(G_t\) is about **strategic insight**; errors can be catastrophic but are often **hard to attribute**.  
- \(V\) can be high in principle but is constrained by access, classification, and politics.  
- \(M\) includes **double agents** and **controlled sources**; adversary actively manipulates \( \theta_S \).

Result:  
- High risk of **systematic fabrication** (or controlled deception) that persists because:  
  - the source has unique access,  
  - the case officer becomes an advocate,  
  - and the institution overweights the value of that access.

### 5. Design levers as game‑theoretic interventions

You can now frame policy or organizational reforms as **changes to game parameters**:

- **Increase \(V\):**  
  - Invest in analytic and technical verification; mandate multi‑source corroboration.  
  - In intel: institutionalize red‑teaming and CI review of key sources.  
  - In LE: require independent corroboration for major operations driven by CI tips.

- **Strengthen \(S_c\):**  
  - Clear, enforced consequences for fabrication (for both sources and handlers who ignore red flags).  
  - Shared blacklists and reliability indices across units/agencies.

- **Re‑shape \(P\):**  
  - Reward **quality‑adjusted output** (e.g., verified cases, low error rates) rather than raw counts.  
  - Give career credit for **calling out bad sources** and preventing bad ops.

- **Modify the contract form:**  
  - Shift from “pay per story” to “pay per verified contribution over time.”  
  - Use non‑cash support (treatment, relocation, structured debt relief) to reduce \(\alpha(D)\) and lengthen the informant’s time horizon.
